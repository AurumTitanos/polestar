# Polestar UI brief

The design brief for the addon's own surfaces (`addon/Polestar/UI`, styled by `addon/Polestar/Theme`). DESIGN.md holds the tokens and components; this file holds the intent they serve.

Scope: the options window, the guide picker, and the on-screen guide window (standard and transparent modes) of the Polestar addon.

Audience and job: Forever players mid-session; set up once in options, pick or switch guides in the picker, then read the guide window at a glance for hours.

## Direction

THESIS: A guiding-star instrument, not a utility panel. Every Polestar surface reads like one crafted object: a night-sky header band carrying the Polestar star emblem, then quiet, precisely gridded controls. Refuses the default addon look of grey boxes, stacked full-width rows and eyebrow labels.

WORLD: Night grounds that never go flat grey, lifted by default. The default scheme is Dusk: Night's navy lifted to dusk (window ground #181D2D, panels a step above, sidebar and footer a step below), clearly lighter but still a night world under a starlit band. Schemes are the user's choice: Dusk (default), Night (the original deep navy-black, unchanged), Graphite (neutral, lighter), and EllesmereUI (its panel colour and accent) offered only while EllesmereUI is installed; it is an option, never a lock. On top of any scheme the user may set their own accent, ground colour, ground opacity and a brightness that lifts or lowers every ground together; every colour resolves own value > Match EllesmereUI > scheme, and everything repaints live. Kept in every scheme: a faint starfield and constellation hairline in header bands, one accent for interactive state (Polestar gold by default; EllesmereUI's while following it), a light text ladder (100/70/50) tinted toward the ground, 1px hairlines, an authored glyph icon family, Inter for the UI (or the game's or EllesmereUI's font by choice), and the engraved serif wordmark for the identity only. Legibility is an invariant: primary text at 7:1 and secondary text, muted text and subject colours at 4.5:1 on every ground of every scheme; a custom ground too light for the ladder is darkened until it reads. No light scheme: transparent mode and the arrow put text straight on the game world and the starfield band has no daylight form.

STORY: The player understands where they are (guide, step, progress) without reading. They trust it because every control looks deliberate, and they act with one click: switch tab, pick guide, change a setting.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Options at 960x680 on one grid shared by every shell. A 240px sidebar with its own compact rhythm (no gap under the logo): emblem and wordmark centred on the page title's line, the search 14px under the brand, the navigation 12px under the search, all on the 24px edge, nav rows with icons and a 2px accent bar on the active page (rows with nothing in them fade and drop their count), the version at the bottom. The main column (left edge 32) has a 112px starfield header with the page title at a fixed place and a muted subtitle and the close at top right (no scale control: menu scale lives under Appearance), then a 40px tab row with an accent underline (left out when a page has one tab), then a two-column grid of label-left/control-right rows 40px high under small section headers, and a footer bar with reset/reload on the left and Close on the right at matching insets; a shell without footer actions drops the bar. The emblem watermark appears only behind empty states. Appearance holds the colour scheme, accent, background colour, brightness, background opacity, the follow-EllesmereUI toggles (when installed), font, menu scale and reduced motion; right-click resets any colour or slider. The hub (the window the guide menu grew into, docs/SUITE.md) uses the same shell with a section rail in front of the sidebar: a 64px rail on the sidebar ground, the emblem on the title's line at its top, then one button per section, the first level with the sidebar's first row (18px glyph over an 11px label, 56px rows, the active one with the selection wash, a 2px accent bar at the window's edge and the accent glyph), Options at the rail's foot, its label on the line of the sidebar's version text; it holds eight sections at 680 tall. The sections: Guides, Lookup, Dungeons and Rares, then Options; a section with one view shows itself as the sidebar's single row. The 240px sidebar then holds the wordmark, the hub search directly under it (driven by the active section when it searches) and that section's own index: the guide categories under Guides, Lookup's kinds, Dungeons' level bands, Rares' lists. Sections own their frame over the list and details panes; their top-level divisions live in the sidebar, not in a tab row; a tab row under the header is only for views inside one division. The guide window: a tab strip on top, a header with the guide title, a step navigator (prev / n of N / next) and a slim progress bar, the step title, goals with icon, subject colour and right-aligned counters, and upcoming steps collapsed. Transparent mode removes every fill and border and keeps outlined text; chrome appears on hover.

FORM: the bar is a polished, full-fidelity options window (a clear sidebar, a titled header, tabbed pages, every control aligned to one grid and explained), executed in Polestar's own identity.

READING FLOW (supersedes the guide window and picker parts of FIRST VIEWPORT): one thing to do at a time, read at a glance, the next step always named, in Polestar's own identity.
- Guide window: a compact instrument. Tab strip; a slim navigator row (prev, the step number, next, menu); then ONE focus at a time: the person to talk to now (icon, name in NPC colour, where) with only that person's goals under it, or, for a field step, its few objectives. One line per goal, the counter right after the text ("Kill Zhevra Chargers 3/8") in the text colour (success once complete; the accent is kept for the current goal's bar and glyph, tabs, primary buttons and progress), done goals checked and dimmed. A single dim "Then: Gazrog" line names the next person; "Person 2 of 8" counts them. A guide progress bar with percent at the bottom; in transparent mode its track stays faintly visible. Tips collapse to one line, opened on demand; Up next is off by default. Larger, higher-contrast type than before: goal text 14px white, names in their kind colour, nothing below 11px anywhere (chips included).
- Guide menu (the window the player opens): three panes on the shell grid, its lines continuous across them: the zone art's bottom edge, and the tab divider at y=152; one left edge at 32 in the middle pane (folder headers, row titles, cards, empty states), state glyphs in the gutter. Left: the hub rail (sections, Options at its foot), then the sidebar with the wordmark, search and categories with icons (a category with no guide for any faction is left out; one empty only for this faction fades). Middle: tabs Home (continue and suggested), Browse (the categories and search), Current (open tabs), Recent, one always active, with the faction filter at the right of the tab row; the header subtitle says something new (faction, count, open tabs); one line per guide, title with its level range in parentheses, a check on finished guides and one "Tab N" note on open ones, grouped under small folder headers; the list runs to the window's bottom (no hint bar: the load hint is the row tooltip). Right: the selected guide's details: zone art for zones with the title on the painting, chips, progress, then a facts block of real guide data (steps, quests, zones in order, the guide after it), then pitch, pros and cons, with "Load guide" ("Continue" at full width when it is open) and "Open in new tab" pinned at the bottom. Selecting shows details; loading is explicit.
- Route choice: each card leads with the zone art, the name and one stat row (levels, travel time, quests) at the same height on every card, then the pitch and at most three pros and two cons, one line each (the rest in the tooltip).

DUNGEONS (quests, bosses and entrances; no loot or BiS browsing): the hub section "Dungeons" is a dungeon quest journal in Polestar's world: dungeons grouped by level, a page per dungeon with its quests (giver, objective, chain, rewards), bosses and entrance. Sidebar: For your level (default; a dungeon whose quests are all the other faction's is not yours), All dungeons, 10–20 … 50–60 and Raids, with counts; the faction filter as segments at the right of the tab row, yours first, on Home and pages. Home: two-column cards in level order, a 72px zone painting centred on the entrance with the name on its fade, one stats line (levels, zone, quests for the filter; never "0 quests") and chips only where true (Forever, Your level). Page: the zone painting fills the header band with the title on it, a back chevron in the gutter, the accent on "Guide me to the entrance" only ("Show on map" secondary); tabs Quests n, Bosses n, Map. Quests: one block per quest between hairlines, everything visible: the name in the client's difficulty colour lifted to 4.5:1, the state glyph in the gutter and a state chip (Done, Ready to turn in, In your log, From level n), level, requirement and flags; a label column with Objective, Starts and Ends side by side with arrow pins, the Chain as muted-bullet rows (a check when done) with a pin after each place, Choose one / You get item tiles in quality borders whose hover is the game tooltip, then XP, money and reputation chips. Bosses: kill order in the gutter, name, level and the quests each serves (a line opens its quest). Map: the entrance and nearby quest givers on the zone painting. No loot tables. Speed is part of the design: cards paint their art only in view, quest blocks are planned from data and drawn only in view, every row is pooled.

FINISH: a surface is finished when it has been reviewed against this brief and DESIGN.md.
